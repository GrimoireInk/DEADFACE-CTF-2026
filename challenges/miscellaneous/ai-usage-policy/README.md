## 1. Overview

This entry documents the Artificial Intelligence (AI) usage policy established by DEADFACE CTF staff for the 2026 competition.

Unlike traditional Capture the Flag challenges, this task was not intended to test technical exploitation, reverse engineering, cryptography, or digital forensics.

Instead, its purpose was to communicate the competition's rules regarding AI-assisted participation and ensure competitors understood the distinction between **permitted AI assistance** and **prohibited autonomous AI-driven solutions**.

---

## 2. Competition AI Policy

Competitors were prohibited from using AI agents, autonomous AI tools, or automated AI-driven systems to independently solve challenges.

The competition was intended to evaluate human knowledge, technical ability, investigative reasoning, and problem-solving skills.

### Permitted AI Usage

The policy allowed AI-powered tools to assist with:

- General research and learning
- Understanding technical concepts
- Troubleshooting
- Guidance during investigations

These uses were permitted provided that the competitor remained responsible for performing the actual challenge-solving work.

### Prohibited AI Usage

The policy prohibited:

- Autonomous AI agents independently solving challenges
- Submitting flags or solutions obtained primarily through AI-generated work
- AI interaction with the competition's scoring server or infrastructure

The distinction emphasized that AI could serve as an educational resource but could not replace the competitor's own investigative effort.

---

## 3. Enforcement & Consequences

The DEADFACE CTF staff reserved the right to investigate suspected violations of the AI usage policy.

According to the challenge instructions, potential consequences included:

1. Disqualification from the competition
2. Removal from the leaderboard
3. Ineligibility for prizes or awards

Competition staff retained final authority regarding investigations and enforcement decisions.

---

## 4. Cybersecurity Ethics & Responsible AI Usage

This policy highlights an important principle in cybersecurity education: **tools should support skill development rather than substitute for technical understanding**.

CTF competitions provide opportunities to develop practical experience in areas such as:

- Digital forensics
- Reverse engineering
- Network analysis
- Cryptography
- Web and application security
- Problem-solving and investigative reasoning

AI-assisted learning can help competitors understand unfamiliar techniques, but authentic skill development depends on performing and understanding the investigative process.
